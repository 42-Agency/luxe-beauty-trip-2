How to Plan a K-Beauty Trip to Seoul, Part Three: Doing Your Research

August 2026  ·  6 min read

By now you have a treatment plan from Part One and you understand the three clinic tiers from Part Two. This is the part that actually protects you: how to research a specific clinic before you trust it with your face. Seoul has thousands of clinics and the good ones do not always market the loudest. Here is how to tell the difference.

1. Start Where Koreans Start, Not Where Google Sends You

Search in Korean

If you search in English on Google, you mostly see clinics that pay to be found. Korean patients do not research this way. They use Naver, the search engine most of the country actually uses, and two review apps built specifically for cosmetic procedures.

  • Gangnam Unni is the largest Korean app for aesthetic and dermatology reviews and booking. It has partial English support and is the closest thing to a single place to compare clinics, doctors, and real patient photos.
  • Babitalk is the other major Korean review app. It is mostly Korean-only, but it is worth translating your way through because the review volume is high and the before-and-after photos are less curated.
  • Naver blogs and cafes are where Korean women write long, honest write-ups of their appointments. Search the clinic name in Korean and read what comes back through a translator.

Use Papago, Naver's translation app, rather than Google Translate for anything in Korean. It handles clinic and treatment language noticeably better. Translate the clinic name into Korean first, then search with that.

2. Verify Who Is Actually Treating You

In South Korea, any licensed doctor can legally perform cosmetic procedures. They do not have to be a dermatologist or a plastic surgeon. That is not automatically a problem, plenty of excellent cosmetic doctors trained a different route, but you should know who is in the room and decide for yourself what matters to you.

The distinction to understand is the board-certified specialist. A doctor who completed a residency and passed the specialty board in dermatology or plastic surgery can display that on their signage and profile. In Korean, look for the phrase meaning "dermatology specialist" or "plastic surgery specialist" rather than just a clinic name ending in the word for clinic.

  • Ask directly: will a specialist perform my treatment, or a general physician? Ask for the name of the doctor, not just the clinic.
  • Once you have a name, search that name on the review apps. Good doctors have a track record you can find. A clinic with strong reviews but no named doctor attached to them is a flag.
  • For anything involving a needle or a laser near your eyes, this matters more. For a basic skin booster, it matters less.

3. Read Reviews Like a Skeptic

Review volume in Korea is enormous, but a lot of it is incentivised. Clinics routinely offer a discount or a free add-on in exchange for a review, so a wall of recent five-star posts tells you the clinic runs a review promotion, not that it is the best in the city.

What to actually look for:

  • Specificity. Useful reviews name the doctor, the device, the shot count, the price paid, and how recovery went. Vague praise is worth nothing.
  • Consistency over time. A steady stream of detailed reviews across two or three years beats a sudden burst in the last month.
  • The photos. Before-and-after images are often shot under different lighting and angles, and some are edited. Look for consistent framing and unretouched skin texture.
  • The complaints. Read the three-star reviews, not the five-star ones. That is where you learn how the clinic handles a result that did not go to plan.

4. Confirm the Device and the Product Are Genuine

Ask to See It

Counterfeit and unlicensed machines exist in the Seoul market, and so does the practice of reusing single-use parts. This is rare at reputable clinics and common enough at the cheapest ones that you should check.

  • Ask for the exact device name and manufacturer before you book. Genuine Thermage, Ultherapy, and similar systems use single-use tips that are sold in fixed shot counts.
  • For those treatments, ask the clinic to open the sealed tip in front of you and confirm the shot count matches what you paid for. A good clinic does this without being asked.
  • For injectables such as Rejuran, ask to see the sealed box. Check the product name matches what was quoted.
  • If a clinic is evasive about which machine it uses, or the price is far below everyone else for the same treatment, walk away.

5. Get Pricing in Writing, in Korean Won

Seoul clinics advertise "event" pricing, which is promotional pricing that changes constantly and often applies only to a package or a first visit. The number you see on an ad is rarely the number you pay.

  • Ask for a written quote in Korean won, itemised by treatment and shot count, before you travel. Convert it yourself.
  • Prices are usually quoted before 10 percent VAT. Some clinics registered for medical tourism can process a partial VAT refund for foreign visitors, so ask about that in advance.
  • Expect the in-person consultation to surface add-ons that were not in the quote. Decide your budget and your priorities before you sit down, so you can say no to the upsell.
  • Get the same treatment quoted by two or three clinics. It tells you the real market rate and shows you who is padding.

6. Lock Down Language Before You Book, Not on Arrival

Do not assume English support. Confirm it in writing when you book, and be specific: will there be an English-speaking coordinator, or an English-speaking doctor, and which parts of the visit are covered.

Ask for your treatment plan in writing before you arrive, with the treatments, the doctor, the devices, and the price. If a clinic will not put that in an email, that tells you how the rest of the visit will go.

Red Flags to Walk Away From

Same-day pressure
A hard push to book and pay during the consultation, before you have had time to think
Price far below market
The same treatment quoted at a fraction of what three other clinics charge
No named doctor
Strong clinic branding but no individual physician you can look up
Vague about devices
Will not name the machine, or will not open sealed parts in front of you

None of this needs to take days. An hour on the review apps, one clear email exchange with each clinic on your shortlist, and a written quote is enough to tell the careful clinics from the careless ones.
