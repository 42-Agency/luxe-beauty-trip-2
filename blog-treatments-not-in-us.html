Skin Treatments You Can't Get in the US, But Can in Seoul

May 2026  ·  6 min read

If you've been researching Korean skin care, you've probably noticed the same names keep appearing: Rejuran, Juvelook, exosome therapy, Innotox. These aren't trends. They are clinically established treatments performed daily at Seoul's top dermatology clinics, and they are essentially unavailable in the United States. Not because American doctors don't know about them, but because they have not been approved by the FDA. For North American women who have hit a ceiling with what their local med spa can offer, it is one of the most compelling reasons to consider a Korea beauty trip.

1. Rejuran Healer (PDRN Injection)

Only in Korea

What it is

Rejuran is a polynucleotide (PN) injection derived from salmon DNA, part of the family of treatments often labelled PDRN. It works by activating your skin's own repair mechanisms. Unlike fillers that add volume, Rejuran regenerates the skin itself, improving texture, elasticity, fine lines, and hydration from within. It's considered a foundational treatment in Korean dermatology, often described as "skin rehabilitation" rather than cosmetic correction.

Why you can't get it in the US

Polynucleotide injectables like Rejuran have not received FDA approval for use in the United States, so no US med spa or dermatologist can legally administer them. In Korea, Rejuran has been widely used since 2014 with an extensive clinical track record. Many Korean women have it done annually as preventative maintenance, the equivalent of a routine dental clean.

What to expect

Most patients see meaningful improvement in skin quality, refined texture, and reduced fine lines after 2–3 sessions spaced 3–4 weeks apart. Results are gradual and cumulative rather than immediate.

2. Juvelook: Next-Generation Skin Booster

Only in Korea

What it is

Juvelook is a Korean-developed injectable that combines hyaluronic acid with poly-D,L-lactic acid (PDLLA) microspheres. The HA provides immediate hydration while the PDLLA microspheres dissolve slowly over months, continuously stimulating new collagen. Most standard fillers do one or the other. Juvelook does both at once, which makes it particularly effective for overall skin quality rather than targeted volume correction.

Why you can't get it in the US

Juvelook is not FDA-approved. It is approved by Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, CE-marked in Europe, and used across Asia, but it has not entered the North American market and there is no clear timeline for when it will.

What to expect

Plumper, more hydrated skin immediately, followed by progressive collagen stimulation over 3–6 months. Often described as restructuring the skin's foundation rather than topping it up.

3. Exosome Therapy

Only in Korea

What it is

Exosomes are nano-sized vesicles packed with growth factors, peptides, and cell-signalling proteins. Applied topically immediately after microneedling, the serum penetrates deep through the micro-channels the needles have created, which accelerates healing, reduces redness, and amplifies the regenerative results of the treatment. Most clinics apply a standard calming mask post-microneedling. Korean clinics use exosome serums instead. It's a meaningful upgrade.

Why you can't get it in the US

No exosome product has FDA approval in the United States, and the FDA treats applying them after microneedling as administering an unapproved drug. Korean clinics have used clinical-grade exosome serums for years, produced under Korean manufacturing and quality standards, which is why women flying to Seoul can add a step their local med spa cannot legally offer.

What to expect

Faster healing post-treatment, reduced inflammation, and enhanced regeneration. Most often used as an add-on to maximize the results of another procedure done on the same day.

4. Innotox: Liquid Botulinum Toxin

Only in Korea

What it is

Innotox is a liquid form of botulinum toxin type A made by the Korean company Medytox. Conventional toxins such as Botox and Dysport arrive as a freeze-dried powder that a nurse mixes with saline right before your appointment. Innotox comes pre-mixed in a standardized solution, which practitioners like for consistent dosing from one vial to the next. It is used the same way as any type A toxin, softening forehead lines, frown lines, and crow's feet, along with jaw slimming and the light micro-dosing technique often called skin botox that is popular in Seoul.

Why you can't get it in the US

Innotox has been approved by Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety since 2013, but Medytox has never filed it with the FDA, so no US clinic can offer it. American practices use other type A toxins, Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau, and Daxxify, all of which are reconstituted powders rather than a ready-made liquid.

What to expect

Onset within a few days, full effect at about two weeks, and results that last roughly three to four months, in line with other type A toxins.
